Only months present in both years, with enough full swings on each side, are compared — that is what makes it like for like. A club playing 12 yards further in July than in March tells you about the ground, not about you; July against July does not have that problem. Every club is listed either way — one that cannot be compared says why, since a club leaving or joining the bag is worth seeing rather than silently missing from the table.

How the seasonal figures are worked out

A club's raw average mixes full swings with punch-outs and layups, and those partials are not evenly spread through the year. Left alone they produce nonsense — on the raw numbers January comes out as the longest month of your year.

So the seasonal views count a shot only if it is at least 70% of that club's 90th-percentile distance — a rough but effective stand-in for "went at it properly". Every club gets that treatment, wedges included, because each is measured against itself.

The one exception is the pooled Distance Through the Year figure, which mixes clubs together and so leaves wedges out. A full wedge is a vaguer idea than a full 7 iron, and that vagueness would wash into a number whose entire job is to hold steady.
